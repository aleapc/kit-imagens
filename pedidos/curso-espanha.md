**21 imagens.** Ambiente: Espanha peninsular (Madri, Barcelona, Sevilha, Valência, San Sebastián): bar de bairro, mercado, metrô, praça.

Entregar em `entregues/curso-espanha/`, cada arquivo com o **id exato** abaixo, `.webp`, 1200×800, sem nenhum texto na imagem.

## Bloco de estilo — cole ANTES de cada prompt, sem editar

> Pintura digital cinematográfica, luz de fim de tarde (golden hour), cores quentes e saturadas, atmosfera acolhedora e convidativa, estilo das ilustrações de viagem. Sem nenhum texto, letra, número ou logotipo na imagem. Enquadramento horizontal 3:2 (1200×800), profundidade de campo suave, composição com um ponto focal claro. Pessoas de costas, de perfil ou a média distância — nenhum rosto reconhecível em primeiro plano. **Bag, phone, cash, loo — the first hour**

### b08.webp
**Stopping a stranger, and understanding the answer**

Four in the afternoon, your phone is on four per cent, the street you want is not where the map said it was, and forty people a minute are walking past you.

### b10.webp
**Every sign in Spain that has been lying to you**

Ten past one, a glass door with one word painted on it at eye height, three people waiting behind you, and your shoulder is already against the half that does not move.

### b15.webp
**Half a kilo of that, and can I try it on?**

Half nine on a Saturday, the market stall in front of you is three deep, the scoop is already in her hand, and your hand is halfway to the tomatoes.

### b18.webp
**The last morning, and the jacket you left in the taxi**

Twenty to eight in the morning, the case is already in the taxi boot, and your phone is on the counter of the bar where you had the last coffee.

### i02.webp
**Nine seconds at the bar**

Half nine at night, four deep at a bar with no queue in it, your money already in your hand, and the pair who walked in after you are on their second drink.

### i03.webp
**The dish you were never going to order**

Ten past two on a Tuesday, a bar two streets off the square, the waiter reels off three dish names in one breath and stands there with the pad open.

### i04.webp
**The night doesn't start when you think it does**

Half nine on a Thursday, you are out, and the square you crossed at eight is filling up instead of emptying — by half ten there are children running about in it and every table outside is taken.

### i05.webp
**The five questions everyone asks you**

Second round at a counter in a town you got to this morning, the man on the next stool has turned half round, and he has just asked you where you are from.

### i06.webp
**Where you went yesterday, and where he sends you tomorrow**

Nine at night, your third evening in the same bar, and the man who served you on Tuesday puts the glass down, leans on his elbows and asks what you did today.

### i07.webp
**Estaba buenísimo — and the cook comes out**

Half eleven at night, the plates went ten minutes ago, the card machine is on its way to your table, and the woman who cooked what you have just eaten is four steps away behind a bead curtain.

### i08.webp
**Someone invites you home**

Twenty past ten at a bar you have been in all evening, the man on the next bar stool says come and eat at ours on Saturday, and there is about one minute before the round breaks up and nobody has said a time.

### i09.webp
**An afternoon doing what they do**

Ten past six on a Thursday, two streets up from the sand, your shirt still in your hand, and the square in front of you filling up with people who live here.

### i10.webp
**The coins you leave, and the second time you walk in**

Ten past four on a Sunday, three of you at the end of a long lunch, twenty-eight sixty on the little plate, and everybody has gone quiet and is waiting to see what somebody puts on top of it.

### a01.webp
**How they talk when it isn't to you**

Nine at night, third in at the bar with your elbow on the zinc: the man next to you orders, the woman behind the bar answers, and neither sentence has an S left in it — and none of it is for you.

### a02.webp
**What they laugh at — and the one joke you can make**

Ten at night, six round a table, the man opposite has just called his oldest friend a donkey to his face, everybody is laughing — and the one person they are watching is you.

### a03.webp
**Who they think they are**

Ten at night on a terrace in a town you cannot pronounce, the man who poured the wine has asked where you are from, and his own answer to that question is a village forty minutes up the road.

### a05.webp
**Bon dia, kaixo, bo día**

Ten past nine on a Tuesday in Gràcia, the board behind the counter is in a language you half recognise, four regulars have looked up, and the barman is waiting for you to say something.

### a06.webp
**August, the saint, and the week the street belongs to everyone**

Quarter past four on a Tuesday in August, twenty-five minutes on foot behind you, a metal shutter down, and the woman in the bar next door watching you read the card taped to it.

### a07.webp
**Two kisses, one hand, and knowing when to go**

Half eleven at night, the plates went an hour ago, you have twice said you ought to be going, and the man beside you has just put a hand on your arm and started another story.

### a08.webp
**So that they ask you back**

Half eleven on your last night, the table has been saying goodbye for ten minutes and nobody has stood up, and the man who poured your coffee every morning is behind the bar.
